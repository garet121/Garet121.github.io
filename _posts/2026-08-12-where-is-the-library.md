I work as a product manager in a very large tech company, where I sometimes describe myself as "an English major who wandered too far from the library." My company began 2026 with an attitude of "AI everywhere!" Though we never quite adopted the tokenmaxxing leaderboard nonsense, more AI was definitely considered better than less. That enthusiasm faded with the advent of usage-based billing, and I would describe our current AI posture as more thoughtful and moderated. Still bullish, certainly, but now with one eye on the price tag.

I myself have a lot of reservations about AI. I think AI skeptics make great points: it's a technology developed and promoted by oligarchs; it's trained on work stolen from artists and writers; and it was sold to capital with the promise that if they buy enough of it they might not have to pay for labor (though you hear this last claim less every day). And the environmental impacts mean I definitely do not want a data center in my neighborhood.

But the inescapable truth is, my industry has bet the farm on AI, and failing to master it is a threat to my career growth — or even my continued employment. So here I am, learning how to make the most of AI, hopefully as Corey Doctorow's metaphorical [centaur](https://doctorow.medium.com/https-pluralistic-net-2025-09-11-vulgar-thatcherism-there-is-an-alternative-f1428b42a8fd) — becoming more effective *myself*, and not being a body for a computer to use while it does all the thinking.

### Guardrails

It makes sense to establish some parameters for this project. I'm primarily interested in use cases that:

- make me generally more effective at my job
- reallocate my mental and emotional labor away from the trifling and toward more worthy investments
- facilitate better record-keeping and better accountability
- allow me to be more present in meetings: to ask better follow-up questions, focus on what's not being said, identify sleeper issues before they become problems (the things I miss when my focus is on taking notes)

And I will try to avoid use cases that:

- automate away critical thinking
- mistake activity for progress
- produce reams of documents that go unused

Finally, I pride myself on being a capable writer. What I share here will be my own writing, typed with my own human fingers, though I see no problem using AI as an editor. If you have a different point of view, I welcome respectful dialogue on the subject.

Next time, I'll introduce ECHO (*Executive Context and Heuristics Organizer*), my first agentic system, which I began developing in April and have been iterating on since. It was built with the above parameters in mind and has been doing an increasingly capable job, both of helping me manage my workload *and* serving as a worthy object lesson.
